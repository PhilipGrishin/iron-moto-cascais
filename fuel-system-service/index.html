02 · Why carburettors stop working

A carburettor has no electronics to fail; what fails is the fuel and the passages it flows through. Petrol left standing evaporates within weeks and leaves gum and varnish behind. Pump petrol in Europe contains up to 10 % ethanol (E10): ethanol absorbs water from the air, separates out at the bottom of the float bowl and corrodes aluminium and zinc alloys into white oxide, while it hardens and swells old rubber. A pilot jet is a few tenths of a millimetre across — it is the first passage to block, and a bike that stood for a few months over winter can already show it.

The symptoms are familiar to anyone who rides a carburetted bike:

  • hard starting, especially cold, and a long warm-up on choke;
  • an idle that hunts, drops or needs the throttle held open;
  • hesitation just off idle, flat spots, popping on the overrun;
  • a smell of petrol, or fuel dripping from the overflow — a float needle that no longer seals;
  • one cylinder cold at idle on a twin or a four, and more vibration than the bike used to have;
  • black spark plugs and consumption that has crept up.

Carburettor cleaner through the bowl drain and a new set of plugs rarely fix any of this for long, because the deposits sit inside drillings a spray cannot reach. That is why we strip the carburettor and put the bare body in the ultrasonic bath.

03 · Ultrasonic cleaning: what happens in the bath

Ultrasonic cleaning works by cavitation. Transducers under the tank vibrate the heated cleaning solution tens of thousands of times per second; microscopic bubbles form and collapse against the metal, and that collapse lifts varnish, gum and oxide out of every drilling, emulsion tube and jet — including the passages no brush, wire or spray can enter. The process is non-abrasive: threads, jet orifices and sealing faces keep their exact geometry, which matters, because a jet opened up with a drill bit is a jet with the wrong number.

We use a professional ultrasonic bath with professional cleaning agents chosen for aluminium carburettor bodies and brass jets — not a household degreaser, which can attack the alloy. After the bath every passage is blown through with compressed air and checked against the light.

What never goes into the bath. Rubber O-rings, diaphragms, floats, float needles with rubber tips, gaskets, plastic parts and anything electrical — the bath would damage them. That is the reason for complete disassembly: only bare metal is cleaned, and every soft part is renewed or checked by hand.

04 · The full procedure, step by step

1. Intake and first checks. Model, year, history, what the bike does. We check the simple things first — fuel delivery, air filter, vacuum lines, ignition — so that a carburettor is cleaned because it needs to be.

2. Removal and documentation. Carburettors off the engine; jet sizes, needle clip positions and float heights recorded before anything is touched.

3. Complete disassembly. Every jet, emulsion tube, needle, slide, diaphragm, float and screw comes out; soft parts are set aside and never see the bath.

4. Ultrasonic bath. Bare bodies and brass in the heated professional bath with professional cleaning agents; passages blown through and checked.

5. Inspection and estimate. Needle and seat, float-needle tips, slide bores, diaphragms held up to the light, throttle-shaft seals. Worn or failed parts and all sealing rubbers go on a written list — approved by you before they are ordered.

6. Reassembly to specification. New gaskets and O-rings, float heights set, the right jets in the right places, mixture screws to the base setting.

7. Refit and electronic synchronisation. Carburettors back on the engine, warm-up, idle and mixture set, synchronisation with electronic gauges, a test ride, and a final check with the engine hot.

05 · Electronic synchronisation

On an engine with more than one carburettor or throttle body, every cylinder has its own intake, and each must open by exactly the same amount — at idle and as the throttle moves. If one opens earlier, that cylinder carries more of the load: the idle hunts, the bike vibrates, response is rough at small throttle openings and one cylinder runs hotter than its neighbours.

Synchronisation measures the intake vacuum of every cylinder at the same time and adjusts the linkage until the readings match within the manufacturer's tolerance. We do it with electronic gauges, which resolve differences a mechanical gauge cannot show and display the pulsing of each cylinder in real time. It is the last step of every carburettor rebuild here; it is needed after any carburettor or throttle-body removal; and it is worth doing on its own on a bike that runs but idles unevenly — on carburettors and on the throttle bodies of fuel-injected engines alike.

06 · Fuel injectors: more than ten checks on the bench

Injectors fail more quietly than carburettors. Deposits on the nozzle and pintle turn a fine cone of spray into a stream, reduce the flow of one injector against its neighbours and let fuel dribble after the injector should have closed. The engine management compensates up to a point, which is why the symptoms — rough idle, hesitation, higher consumption, a misfire under load — arrive late.

We remove the injectors, clean them ultrasonically and run each one through more than ten checks on the test bench, among them:

  • spray pattern at idle and at higher pulse widths — a cone, not a jet or a dribble;
  • flow at several pulse widths, static and pulsed;
  • delivery compared across the set — the injectors of one engine should be within a few per cent of each other;
  • leak-tightness under system pressure with the injector closed;
  • coil resistance and opening response at low duty;
  • a before-and-after comparison, so you can see what changed.

Filters and seals are renewed where needed. An injector that cannot be brought back to specification is named as such, with the price of a replacement in writing.

FAQ

How much does carburettor cleaning cost?⌄

One carburettor from 75 €, two from 150 €, four from 200 € — the work on the carburettor itself: strip-down, ultrasonic cleaning, inspection, reassembly. Removal from the bike, refitting and synchronisation are quoted separately, and so are sealing kits and any worn parts, per carburettor — all in one written estimate you approve before anything is ordered. Fuel injectors: written estimate by model.

How long does it take?⌄

It depends on the number of carburettors, their condition and which parts have to be ordered. We don't quote a turnaround before the carburettors are on the bench; once they are open, you get a date together with the estimate.

Can I bring just the carburettors?⌄

Yes, for cleaning and rebuilding. Synchronisation and the final setting need the engine running, so the last step happens with the bike here — or you refit them yourself and come in for synchronisation alone.

Is ultrasonic cleaning safe for a carburettor?⌄

For the bare aluminium body and the brass parts, yes: cavitation is non-abrasive, and the bath runs with professional agents meant for these alloys. Rubber, diaphragms, floats, plastics and anything electrical never go in — which is why we disassemble completely first.

Do you service fuel-injected bikes too?⌄

Yes: throttle-body synchronisation, injector removal, ultrasonic cleaning and more than ten bench checks per injector, plus fuel pumps, filters and lines. Fault diagnostics 50–350 € when the cause isn't clear.

The bike stood all winter and won't start — is it the carburettor?⌄

Often, but not always. Old fuel and a blocked pilot jet are the usual suspects on a carburetted bike; a flat battery, a tired fuel pump or ignition can look the same.
